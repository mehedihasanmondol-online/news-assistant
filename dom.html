Former LeBron James teammate says Lakers will be in Play-In with Luka Doncic

Iman Shumpert believes Luka Doncic and the Los Angeles Lakers will struggle and face a Play-In situation next NBA season.

Iman Shumpert believes that Luka Doncic and the Los Angeles Lakers will struggle next NBA season.

The former NBA veteran made the bold claim in his recent appearance at Gil's Arena.

"I think it's going to be a Play-In type situation. I feel like they [Lakers] going to really be forcing it at the end," Shumpert said.

"Real talk though. I'm just saying it's going to be borderline and it's going to be borderline on some Luka got to pull a rabbit out of a hat, and he needs somebody to lose over here."

Lakers facing doubts amid brand-new group

With the offseason departures of LeBron James and several other key pieces such as Rui Hachimura, Marcus Smart, Luke Kennard, and Jaxson Hayes, the Los Angeles Lakers have taken a significant retooling this offseason.

They retained Austin Reaves via a max contract, landed the new center of their future in Walker Kessler, and tapped new faces such as Sandro Mamukelashvili, Quentin Grimes, Collin Sexton, Kevon Looney, Ziaire Williams, Matisse Thybulle, and Jaden Hardy.

However, despite anticipation for their next year's campaign, the Lakers have been facing plenty of doubts and skepticism around the new-look group.

Even several personnel within the organization believe they got even weaker on paper entering 2026-27.

In the end, it all depends on how the team will perform under Luka Doncic's lead.

Doncic, who is now fully healthy after his season-ending hamstring injury, embraced the new group around him by hosting a team minicamp in Slovenia. He is now poised for a bounce-back year amid offseason focus on his weight and wellness.

As the Western Conference remains competitive as it is, only time will tell how Doncic and the Lakers will fare and live up to the expectations.

Rimas Kurtinaitis resigns as Lithuania's head coach

2026 September 15 20:45

Rimas Kurtinaitis has decided to step down as head coach of the Lithuanian national team alongside his entire coaching staff. Kurtinaitis led Lithuania for nearly two years, highlighted by a fifth-place finish at EuroBasket. A successor will be named following the upcoming Lithuanian Basketball Federation presidential election.

Credit: Robertas Riabovas/BNS
Credit Robertas Riabovas/BNS
Credit Robertas Riabovas/BNS

Add us on

Lithuania's national team head coach, Rimas Kurtinaitis, has decided to step down, the Lithuanian Basketball Federation (ALK) announced.

According to the announcement, Kurtinaitis' entire coaching staff is resigning with him.

Lithuania to announce new head coach after Federation's election

The national team's new head coach will be named following the election of the new president of the Lithuanian Basketball Federation, but no later than the start of preparations for the upcoming World Cup window.

The election is expected to take place either at the end of October or at the beginning of November. 

Kurtinaitis' decision comes after former ALK president Mindaugas Balciunas stepped down on Friday.

As BasketNews reported on Monday, Remigijus Milasius was named the interim ALK president until the election of the new president.

However, Milasius is expected to be named the next ALK president and lead the federation until the end of the summer of 2028.

Rimas Kurtinaitis' stint with Lithuania's national team

Kurtinaitis led the Lithuanian national team for nearly two years, having taken charge in October 2024.

Last year, the national team finished fifth at the EuroBasket under his guidance.

"I have made the decision to part ways with the national team. I believe now is the right time, as the new coaching staff will have enough time to prepare for the upcoming qualification window," Kurtinaitis said in a press release. "I had already made up my mind after the first round of qualifiers, but I was asked to step in and continue through this recent window."

Credit Robertas Riabovas/BNS

"I want to wish the best of luck to the coaching staff that succeeds me and to the entire Lithuanian national team. I also want to sincerely thank my players who stepped up to help during such a crucial time for Lithuanian basketball. A big thank you to the entire national team staff, the assistant coaches, and all the fans who supported the team through this challenging stretch," Kurtinaitis added.

Lithuania in danger of missing FIBA World Cup

Lithuania has not failed to qualify for the FIBA World Cup since 2002, yet that possibility is very real.

Lithuania sit fourth in Group J at 4-4, behind Turkiye (8-0), Serbia (6-2) and Italy (5-3).

The August window produced a damaging 94-66 defeat against Turkiye before Lithuania recovered to beat Bosnia 78-73.

The problem is that Lithuania has no head-to-head advantage over Italy and won't face them again – their results against Italy carried over from the first round.

Instead, they need to gain ground while navigating games against teams from the other qualifying group.

November window will start with the matchup against Serbia in Lithuania, followed by a potentially vicious road game against Bosnia.
